Identifying Ponzi-like tokens through Blockchain Transaction Networks on Ethereum

Supervised by Professor Flávio Pinheiro

Read the PDF Official version · NOVA repository

In short

Ponzi schemes on Ethereum usually hide inside smart contracts, and reading every contract's code doesn't scale. This thesis asks a simpler question: can you spot them just by looking at how money moves around them?

Each contract is treated as a network of transactions: who sends money to it, who it pays out to, and how that changes over time. From those networks I built features that describe a contract's behaviour, then trained machine learning models to tell Ponzi-like contracts apart from legitimate ones.

The result: a handful of behavioural signals carry most of the information, such as the balance between money coming in and going out, and how quickly new senders stop arriving. That signal holds up even when the size of the contract is taken out of the picture.

What's inside

Data1,190 Ethereum contracts, 167 of them Ponzi schemes, after removing contracts with fewer than five unique senders.
MethodTransaction-network features chosen by stability voting, tested with Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost and SVM.
Key featuresThe ratio of money in to money out, in-degree, out-degree, and how fast new senders decay.
ChecksA sensitivity analysis showing the signal isn't just contract size, and an ablation study showing each feature adds its own information.
LimitationERC-20 token transfers weren't included in the data, which partly explains why the in/out ratio dominates.
